# User Story: Collection Manager

## 1. Persona

**Name:** Thomas

**Role:** Collection Manager, Entomological Collection

**Description:**
Thomas is responsible for the operational management, documentation, and quality assurance of an entomological collection. He maintains and develops collection management systems, coordinates digitization activities, and ensures that specimen information is accurate, complete, and accessible.

He works closely with curators, collection scientists, collection managers, and researchers to support the long-term preservation, documentation, and usability of the collection. A key aspect of his work involves integrating information from multiple sources, including collection databases, historical catalogues, scientific publications, archives, and specimen labels.

Thomas is particularly interested in identifying missing, incomplete, or inconsistent information and improving the overall quality, completeness, and discoverability of collection records. He considers type specimen catalogues to be valuable sources of collection knowledge because they provide expert-reviewed documentation of collection holdings at specific points in time. These catalogues often contain information that is missing from collection management systems and can help verify specimen records, enrich metadata, and document changes in scientific understanding over time.

He aims to use information contained in type specimen catalogues to improve collection data quality, support digitization and curation activities, and strengthen the links between physical specimens and their digital representations. By improving the completeness, accessibility, and reliability of collection information, Thomas helps increase the usability, visibility, and long-term value of the collection for research and collection management.

---

## 2. Scenario and Motivation

Thomas wants to improve the quality, completeness, and accessibility of information about the specimens held in his collection. Information about type specimens is often distributed across collection management systems, historical catalogues, scientific publications, archives, and specimen labels. As a result, important information may remain inaccessible, incomplete, or disconnected from the corresponding specimen records.

Type specimen catalogues are particularly valuable because they contain expert-reviewed and published information about collection holdings at specific points in time. They often provide details that are not available in current collection management systems and can serve as authoritative references for specimen documentation.

Currently, integrating and maintaining information from multiple sources is challenging. Relevant information is frequently scattered across legacy documentation, historical publications, collection databases, and other resources. Identifying and extracting information manually is time-consuming and makes it difficult to maintain complete and consistent collection records.

A **Type Specimen Knowledge Base** would provide structured access to information contained in type specimen catalogues and publications and allow Thomas to connect it with existing collection documentation. The Knowledge Base would support the enrichment of specimen records, improve metadata quality, reveal inconsistencies between information sources, and provide access to historical documentation and expert knowledge.

Using the Knowledge Base, Thomas could:

* Identify information contained in catalogues and publications that is not yet represented in collection management systems.
* Enrich specimen records with additional metadata, identifiers, taxonomic information, locality information, and links to publications and external resources.
* Verify and reconcile information from different sources.
* Identify missing, incomplete, or inconsistent specimen information.
* Strengthen the connections between physical specimens, collection records, and published scientific knowledge.
* Support data quality assurance and collection digitization activities.
* Improve the completeness, consistency, and accessibility of collection data.
* Increase the value, usability, and discoverability of the collection for researchers, collection staff, and biodiversity knowledge infrastructures.

## 3. Competency Questions (CQs)

### CQ1. Type Specimen Documentation

Which type specimens are documented in the available type specimen catalogues and publications, and what information is available for them?

### CQ2. Available Catalogues and Publications

Which type specimen catalogues and publications are available for my institution or collection?

### CQ3. Specialist Contributions

Which authors or specialists have documented type specimens from my collection?

### CQ4. Relevant Publications

Which publications provide information about a particular type specimen or group of type specimens?

### CQ5. Metadata Availability

Which metadata elements (e.g., taxonomy, locality, collector, identifiers, repository information, references, images) are documented for type specimens in the available catalogues and publications?

### CQ6. Documentation Completeness

Which type specimens have the most comprehensive documentation across catalogues and publications?

### CQ7. Catalogue Coverage

Which type specimen catalogues provide the most comprehensive documentation for a collection, taxonomic group, or set of type specimens?

### CQ8. Multiple Documentation Sources

Which type specimens are documented by multiple catalogues or publications?

### CQ9. Historical Changes in Documentation

How has the information available for a particular type specimen changed across catalogues and publications over time?

### CQ10. Identifiers and External Resources

Which type specimens have associated identifiers, collection numbers, or links to external resources?

### CQ11. Richly Connected Specimens

Which type specimens are associated with the richest network of linked information, such as publications, taxonomic names, institutions, collectors, localities, and identifiers?

### CQ12. Information Diversity and Coverage

How does the amount and diversity of information available for type specimens vary across catalogues and publications?
