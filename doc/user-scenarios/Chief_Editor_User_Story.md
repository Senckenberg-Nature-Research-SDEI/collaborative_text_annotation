# User Story: Chief Editor of a Zoological Journal

## 1. Persona

**Name:** Maria

**Role:** Chief Editor, Zoological Journal

**Description:**
Maria is responsible for the scientific content, quality, and strategic development of a zoological journal with a focus on insects. As Chief Editor, she works to increase the visibility, dissemination, and impact of the journal while supporting the mission of the research institute that hosts it as a diamond open-access publication.

Maria is a strong advocate of open access, open data, and the data liberation approach outlined in the Disentis Roadmap. She believes that making data FAIR, discoverable, and reusable increases the value and impact of published research and strengthens the contribution of the journal to the open science ecosystem.

She is responsible for demonstrating the journal's scientific contribution and impact to institute management, scientific advisory boards, and the editorial board. In particular, she seeks to make information on published type specimens accessible, discoverable, and reusable beyond the original publications, as these records represent an important contribution of the journal to biodiversity research. Increasing the visibility and reuse of these data helps strengthen the reputation of the journal, attract authors publishing high-quality research, and contribute to greater recognition, citations, and submissions.

---

## 2. Scenario and Motivation

Maria wants to gain a comprehensive overview of the type specimen information published in her journal and better understand the journal’s contribution to biodiversity knowledge through these scientifically important records. Type specimens are fundamental to biodiversity research because they document and support species concepts. Making this information more visible and accessible helps demonstrate the scientific value and impact of the journal within the research community.

Currently, information about type specimens is embedded within publications and cannot easily be retrieved, aggregated, or analyzed in a structured way. Answering questions about the journal’s type specimen content often requires manually searching publications, working with incomplete or poor OCR text, and compiling information in spreadsheets. As a result, important questions about the journal’s contribution through type specimen information are difficult, time-consuming, or impossible to answer efficiently.

A **Type Specimen Knowledge Base** would provide structured and machine-readable access to type specimen information published in the journal. It would help disseminate the journal’s content beyond the original publications by making type specimen information discoverable through external knowledge systems and biodiversity infrastructures. The Knowledge Base would also support reporting to institute management, scientific advisory boards, and the editorial board while informing strategic planning and future editorial discussions.

Using the Knowledge Base, Maria could:

* Obtain a comprehensive overview of all type specimens published or mentioned in the journal.
* Demonstrate the journal’s contribution to biodiversity knowledge and taxonomy.
* Increase the visibility, discoverability, and reuse of type specimen information.
* Support reporting activities for institute management, advisory boards, and the editorial board.
* Monitor trends and developments in the publication of type specimen information.
* Identify key publications and collections contributing to the journal’s scientific impact.
* Strengthen the reputation of the journal and attract authors publishing high-quality research.
* Support open science principles by making published data more FAIR and reusable.

## 3. Competency Questions (CQs)

### CQ1. Published Type Specimens and Metadata

Which type specimens are published or mentioned in the journal, and what associated metadata are available (e.g., hosting institution, institutional codes, specimen identifiers/PIDs, taxonomic information, locality, sex, and number of specimens)?

### CQ2. Type Specimen Quantification

How many type specimens are represented in the journal?

### CQ3. Collection and Repository Holdings

Which institutions and collections currently hold the type specimens published in the journal?

### CQ4. Taxonomic Distribution

How are the type specimens published in the journal distributed across taxonomic groups (e.g., family, genus, species)?

### CQ5. Geographic Distribution

How are the type specimens published in the journal distributed geographically according to collection localities or gathering sites?

### CQ6. Relevant Publications and Catalogues

Which publications in the journal contain type specimen information, and which publications can be identified as type specimen catalogues?

### CQ7. Publication Context

In which publications and publication sections is information on a specific type specimen reported?

### CQ8. Major Contributors

Which publications contribute the largest number of type specimen records to the journal?

### CQ9. Temporal Trends

How has the publication of type specimen information changed over time within the journal?
